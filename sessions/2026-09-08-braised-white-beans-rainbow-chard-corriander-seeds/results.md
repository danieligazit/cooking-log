# Session Retrospective: 2026-09-08

## Verdict
* **Rating:** 4.0 / 5.0
* Really interesting tastes and flavor combinations.
* Lia liked it!
* Felt a bit maximalist in nature—components were racing each other rather than harmoniously cooperating.
* Lots of room for technical improvement, but an unusual (in a good way) and creative culinary direction.

---

## Reality & Pan Observations
* **Coriander Seeds:** Left whole in this session, which was a clear mistake. The texture was too coarse and jarring, especially given coriander's sharp, floral-citrus profile. They needed to be crushed or ground.
* **Chard Stems:** Ended up noticeably too hard. Even though they cooked longer than the initial recipe suggested, they were still too tough—they need to hit the pan first and stay until they reach a fairly mushy, tender state.
* **Sumac Impact:** Barely felt or noticed in the final dish.
* **Bean Quality:** Canned beans felt functional but lacked character. Cooked dry beans (or higher quality jarred beans) are needed for a proper foundation.

---

## Sensory Debrief & Adjustments for Next Time
* **Chard Stem Strategy:** Cook the stems alone in fat/water under a lid until properly soft before introducing onions/leeks and aromatics.
* **Spice Prep (Coriander):** Always crush or coarsely grind coriander seeds so they bloom into the fat rather than acting as hard pellets.
* **Sumac Level:** Increase the amount significantly or apply it heavily as a raw finishing dust on the plated dish.
* **Bean Choice:** Move to dried beans cooked from scratch with aromatics to build natural, savory bean liquor.
* **Restraint:** Edit back competing elements to give the creamy beans and mineral greens room to breathe.
